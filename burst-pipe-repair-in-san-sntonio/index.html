San Antonio Specific

Why Do Pipes Burst in San Antonio?

San Antonio puts stress on residential plumbing that most cities don't. Here is what causes pipes to fail in this area, and why it happens more than homeowners expect.

01

Hard Water Corrosion

San Antonio's water hardness often exceeds 180 mg/L as calcium carbonate, one of the hardest in the U.S. That mineral load builds scale inside pipes, raises internal pressure, and corrodes copper and brass over time. The result is pinhole leaks, weakened joints, and eventually full pipe failure.

02

Expansive Clay Soil

San Antonio's soil is loaded with clay. It swells when it rains and shrinks when it dries. That constant movement shifts your slab foundation, which cracks rigid pipes, separates joints, and puts stress on buried supply lines. Foundation movement from soil is one of the leading causes of plumbing failure in the area.

03

Winter Freezes

San Antonio doesn't usually freeze hard, and that is exactly why it's dangerous when it does. The February 2021 Winter Storm Uri and the January 2026 arctic event both brought 48-plus hours below freezing, and burst thousands of pipes across Bexar County. Homes here were never built for those conditions. PEX runs in unconditioned attics, copper lines in exterior walls, and exposed hose bibs are the most vulnerable spots every time the temperature drops.

04

Aging Pipe Materials

Older San Antonio homes carry real risk from the pipes they were built with. Galvanized steel pipes from pre-1960s homes corrode from the inside. Polybutylene pipes installed through the mid-1990s become brittle without warning. Copper pipes in homes with hard water develop pinhole leaks over decades. If your home has never been repiped, the original pipes may be at or past the end of their service life.

05

High Water Pressure

Static pressure above roughly 80 psi shortens the life of every joint, supply hose, and valve in your home. A failing pressure-reducing valve (PRV) is a common and overlooked cause of pipe damage. If your PRV is old or failing, your pipes may be taking more stress than they were ever designed to handle.

06

Hot Attic Pipe Runs

Builders in San Antonio historically ran hot water supply lines through attic spaces to save cost. In summer, attic temps can exceed 140 degrees Fahrenheit. That heat degrades copper and PEX over time. In winter, those same attic pipes are the first to freeze. It is the worst of both worlds, and attic pipe failures are one of the most common burst pipe scenarios we see across the metro.

Warning Signs

What Are the Signs of a Burst Pipe?

Sometimes a pipe bursts and water pours out immediately. Other times a pipe cracks slowly and you don't notice it for days. Do not wait for the obvious flood. Watch for these warning signs.

  • Running water sounds with nothing on

    Rushing, dripping, or hissing inside walls or ceilings when all fixtures are off is a strong warning sign.

  • Wet spots or stains on walls and ceilings

    A water stain or soft, bulging area on drywall means water is pooling somewhere it should not be. Act fast.

  • Water pooling on floors

    Especially near cabinets, under sinks, or in the garage. Pooling around pipes can lead to extensive water damage and mold quickly.

  • Sudden drop in water pressure

    If your shower or faucets go weak without explanation, water may be escaping somewhere inside the system.

  • Spike in your SAWS water bill

    If your bill jumps for no clear reason, a hidden leak could be running constantly. Even a small, slow leak adds up fast.

  • Discolored or rust-colored water

    Brown or yellow water from your taps, especially after not using them, indicates internal corrosion and pipe deterioration.

  • Mold or musty smell with no visible source

    Mold begins growing within 24 to 48 hours of water exposure. A musty smell with no obvious cause means moisture is hiding inside your walls or floors.

Spot any of these signs? Do not wait.

What to Do Right Now If a Pipe Bursts

Stay calm. Move fast. Follow these steps in order. Every minute of delay means more water damage spreading through your home.

  1. 1

    Shut Off the Main Water Valve

    This is the single most important thing you can do right now. Find your main shut-off and turn it off completely. This stops water from flowing and limits how far damage spreads. Do not skip this step under any circumstances.

    Can't find it? Call us at (726) 262-0751. We can walk you through it on the phone while we're on the way.
  2. 2

    Open Outdoor Spigots

    Once the main is off, open your outdoor hose bibs and any faucets on the lowest level of your home. This drains the remaining pressure from your lines so water doesn't keep pushing through the crack. It also relieves pressure on any frozen pipes that haven't fully burst yet.

  3. 3

    Turn Off the Water Heater

    With the main shut off, your water heater should go off too. Running a water heater without water flowing through it can damage the unit and create a second problem on top of the first.

  4. 4

    Move Valuables Out of the Water Path

    If water is spreading on the floor or dripping from a ceiling, quickly move electronics, rugs, furniture, and anything else out of the area. Speed matters here. Water travels fast.

  5. 5

    Call a Licensed Plumber Right Now

    This is not a repair you fix with a clamp from the hardware store. A burst pipe inside a wall, ceiling, attic, or under the slab requires the right equipment and experience to locate and fix correctly. Call us now and we will be on the way.

Our Process

How Does Burst Pipe Repair Work?

Every burst pipe situation is a little different. But the repair process follows the same steps every time, so you know exactly what to expect when we arrive.

Locate the Break

Some breaks are obvious. Water is pouring from the wall. Others are hidden deep inside the structure. We use pressure testing, leak detection equipment, and inspection cameras to find exactly where the pipe failed before we open anything up. Precise location means less wall opened and a faster repair.

Cut Out the Damaged Section

Once we locate the break, we remove the damaged section of pipe. We inspect the surrounding pipe for additional weakness or corrosion. If we find soft spots nearby, we address them at the same time. That saves you from a second call within weeks.

Replace With the Right Material

We match the repair to your home's plumbing system. Repair methods include PEX crimp fittings, ProPress flameless joining for copper, and traditional solder joining, depending on pipe type and what the situation calls for. We do not use one-size-fits-all fixes.

Pressure Test the Repair

After the new section is in place, we pressure test the line to confirm the repair is sealed completely. No guessing. No "let's hope it holds." If it doesn't pass the test, we don't leave.

Inspect for Secondary Damage

When a pipe in the ceiling or attic fails, water migrates throughout the assembly before it drips into the living space. We check the affected area for signs that water traveled beyond the visible damage. Catching secondary issues now prevents mold and structural problems later.

Know Your Home

What Type of Pipes Does Your San Antonio Home Have?

The pipe material in your home depends largely on when it was built. Knowing your pipe type helps us repair it correctly and helps you understand the risk level you are dealing with.

Pre-1960s

Galvanized Steel

The protective zinc coating erodes from the inside out, creating corrosion, rust buildup, and flow restriction. Most of these pipes in San Antonio have exceeded their expected service life. If your home has never been repiped, a full inspection is overdue.

High Risk
1960s–1980s

Copper

Durable, but San Antonio's extremely hard water accelerates corrosion. Pinhole leaks in copper lines are common in homes throughout the metro. Hot attic runs are especially vulnerable to heat degradation and freeze damage during hard winter events.

Moderate Risk
Late 1970s–mid-1990s

Polybutylene

Prone to becoming brittle and failing without warning. If your home is from this era and has never been repiped, a single inspection could save you from a major water damage event. Polybutylene is no longer approved for installation anywhere in the U.S.

High Risk
1990s–2000s

CPVC

Chlorinated polyvinyl chloride becomes brittle with age and is vulnerable to certain cleaning chemicals and solvents. Joint connections are the most common failure point. Generally reliable if not exposed to harsh chemicals or thermal stress.

Lower Risk
2000s–Present

PEX

The current standard in San Antonio new construction. More flexible than older materials and can handle a mild freeze. But 48 continuous hours below freezing, like the January 2026 event, can burst PEX fittings and tubing, especially in unconditioned attic spaces.

Lower Risk (freeze-dependent)
Pre-1970s (Drain Lines)

Cast Iron

Cast iron drain lines scale and corrode from the inside over decades. When sections fail, they do not leak a little, they collapse. Common in older San Antonio neighborhoods like Alamo Heights, Monte Vista, and Mahncke Park.

High Risk (if original)

What Affects the Cost of Burst Pipe Repair?

We give you a price before any work starts. You approve it first. But repair costs vary based on several real factors. Here is what goes into the price.

  • Location of the pipe

    A burst pipe under a kitchen sink is fast to reach. A burst pipe inside a wall, attic, or under the slab takes more time and specialized equipment.

  • Accessibility

    If we need to open drywall, tile, or concrete to reach the break, that increases the labor and material involved in the repair.

  • Pipe material

    Different materials require different joining techniques and have different part costs. PEX, copper, CPVC, and galvanized steel each have unique repair requirements.

  • Extent of the damage

    A single clean break repairs faster than a section with multiple cracks or widespread corrosion. We only cut out what is damaged and replace what needs replacing.

  • Time of day

    Emergency service outside regular business hours carries a dispatch fee. We are transparent about this on the call before we send anyone out.

  • Secondary water damage

    If water has been running inside a wall or ceiling for hours, we may need to address water-soaked materials or recommend a restoration team to handle drying and mold prevention.
