Know the Signs

8 Warning Signs Your Water Heater Is Failing

Your water heater usually gives you warning before it completely quits. If you notice any of these, don't wait. Call before a small problem turns into major water damage.

  1. 01

    No Hot Water At All

    On a gas unit, a dead pilot light or failed gas control valve is usually to blame. On an electric unit, a burned-out heating element or tripped breaker is the common cause. Either way, call us. Don't ignore it hoping it fixes itself.

  2. 02

    Hot Water Runs Out Fast

    If you used to get a full hot shower but now run cold in five minutes, sediment is the most likely culprit. Hard minerals from San Antonio's Edwards Aquifer settle at the bottom of the tank and crowd out usable capacity.

  3. 03

    Water Is Scalding Hot

    If your water is dangerously hot even on low, the thermostat is likely stuck or set too high. Safe household water temperature is around 120°F. Above 140°F you risk burns, especially for children and older adults.

  4. 04

    Popping or Rumbling Sounds

    That popping noise from your water heater closet is sediment. The burner or heating element is cooking water trapped under a thick layer of mineral scale. It means your heater is working harder than it should, burning more energy and wearing out faster.

  5. 05

    Rusty or Discolored Hot Water

    Orange or brown hot water is a serious warning. It usually means the inside of your tank is corroding. Once rust sets in, the tank will eventually leak. This is often a sign that replacement is needed, not just repair.

  6. 06

    Water Pooling at the Base

    Any water around the base of your heater is a problem right now. Drips from fittings can sometimes be repaired. But if the leak is from the tank body itself, the tank is corroded through and needs to be replaced. Delay causes floor and wall damage.

  7. 07

    Pilot Light Won't Stay Lit

    On gas units, if the pilot keeps going out, the thermocouple is likely worn. It's a small part that controls whether gas reaches the burner. A faulty thermocouple is one of the most common gas water heater repairs we handle in San Antonio.

  8. 08

    Energy Bills Creeping Up

    If your electric or gas bill keeps climbing but your usage hasn't changed, your water heater may be the reason. A unit coated in sediment runs longer to heat the same water. That wasted energy adds up on every bill.

Seeing any of these? Call us now before the damage spreads.

San Antonio Specific

Why San Antonio Is Especially Tough on Water Heaters

Most cities deal with some mineral deposits in their water. San Antonio is not most cities.

San Antonio's water comes from the Edwards Aquifer, which runs through limestone bedrock deep under Central Texas. By the time it reaches your tap, it carries heavy loads of calcium and magnesium. The San Antonio Water System (SAWS) reports water hardness levels between 16 and 21 grains per gallon. The threshold for "very hard" water is just 7 grains per gallon. San Antonio sits at more than double that, placing it among the hardest municipal water supplies in the country.

Every time your water heater runs, minerals separate out under heat and settle at the bottom of the tank. In San Antonio, this process happens faster than almost anywhere else in the US.

Here Is What That Sediment Does to Your Water Heater

  • Shrinks tank capacity. A 40-gallon unit filled with scale may hold only 20 gallons of usable hot water.
  • Forces the heater to run longer to reach temperature, raising your monthly energy bill.
  • In gas units, sediment sits above the burner and causes the tank bottom to overheat.
  • In electric units, the lower heating element gets buried in scale and burns out early.
  • Accelerates internal corrosion, leading to rust-colored water and eventually tank leaks.
  • Wears down anode rods faster than average, leaving the tank body unprotected.

How often should you flush?

National guidance says once a year. At San Antonio's hardness levels, every six months is smarter. If your unit has never been flushed and is over 8 years old, call a plumber first. The drain valve may be corroded and won't reseal after a DIY attempt.

What about the anode rod?

The anode rod corrodes slowly so your tank does not. Hard water eats through them faster. When it's fully depleted, the tank starts corroding instead. Most homeowners have never had it replaced. We check it on every maintenance visit.

Diagnosis & Repair

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix

We repair all types of water heaters. Select your unit type below to see the specific problems we handle.

Leaking Tank

A leak from a pipe connection or fitting can often be repaired. A leak from the tank body itself means the interior has corroded through and the tank must be replaced. We'll tell you honestly which situation you are in before any work begins.

No Hot Water or Not Enough

We check the pilot light, gas control valve, thermocouple, and thermopile on gas units. On electric units we test both heating elements and both thermostats. We also check for heavy sediment buildup and flush the tank when needed.

Water Temperature Problems

Too hot or not hot enough both point to thermostat failure. We replace faulty thermostats on gas and electric units and set your water temperature to the safe 120°F standard before we leave.

Pressure Relief Valve (T&P Valve)

The T&P valve is a critical safety device. If it drips, leaks, or fails to release when it should, it needs replacement. All T&P work meets City of San Antonio plumbing code, including proper discharge piping requirements.

Sediment Flush

If your tank sounds like a percolator, a professional flush can buy it more years of life. We drain and flush the tank to remove built-up scale. With San Antonio's hard water, this should be done every 6 months for best results.

Anode Rod & Dip Tube

A depleted anode rod leaves your tank open to corrosion. A broken dip tube lets cold water mix with hot near the top, giving you lukewarm water fast. We replace both as needed during repair and maintenance visits.

Ignition Failures

If the unit won't fire at all, the igniter or flame sensor may have failed. We diagnose and replace faulty ignition components across all major tankless brands including Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, and Bosch.

Scale Buildup on Heat Exchanger

Tankless units don't collect sediment like a tank, but San Antonio's hard water builds mineral scale directly on the heat exchanger coils. This causes overheating and shutdowns. We flush and descale with citric acid circulation. In San Antonio, this should happen every 6 to 12 months.

Error Codes

Tankless units display error codes when something is wrong. We diagnose error codes across all major brands and know exactly what each code means so we can get your unit back online fast, often the same day you call.

Flow Sensor Problems

Tankless units need adequate water flow to trigger the burner. A bad flow sensor can prevent the unit from firing even when everything else is fine. We test and replace flow sensors quickly.

Gas Valve Failure

A failing gas valve on a tankless unit causes the burner to not light or to cut out mid-cycle. This is a common repair on older tankless units. We carry gas valve parts for most popular brands.

Annual Descaling Service

Even if nothing seems wrong, annual descaling is essential for tankless units in San Antonio. Skipping it dramatically shortens the life of the heat exchanger. We offer scheduled maintenance so you don't have to remember.

If you smell gas near your water heater, do not try to relight the pilot. Leave the house, leave doors open as you go, and call us from outside. Gas leaks are a life-safety emergency.

Dead or Unstable Pilot Light

If the pilot goes out repeatedly, the thermocouple is almost certainly worn. It's a small sensor that controls gas flow to the burner. It's one of the most common gas heater repairs in San Antonio and one of the quickest to fix.

Thermocouple & Thermopile

The thermocouple keeps the pilot valve open. The thermopile generates the small electrical signal that powers the gas control valve. When either fails, you lose hot water. We test and replace both on all major gas water heater brands.

Gas Control Valve Failure

The gas control valve manages the burner and thermostat together. When it fails, the unit often won't heat at all or heats erratically. We test the valve with proper diagnostic equipment and replace it when needed.

Delayed Ignition or Burner Issues

A "whomp" sound when the burner ignites is a sign of delayed ignition. This is usually caused by a dirty burner orifice or low gas pressure. Left alone, it stresses the heat exchanger and tank. We clean and adjust the burner assembly.

Insufficient Gas Pressure

Low gas pressure from the line reduces burner output, causing slow recovery and lukewarm water. We check line pressure and coordinate with your gas utility if the issue is upstream of the appliance.

Combustion & Venting Problems

Gas heaters need proper venting to exhaust combustion gases safely. A blocked or damaged flue can cause backdrafting, which is dangerous. We inspect venting as part of every gas water heater service call.

Burned-Out Heating Elements

Electric tank heaters have two heating elements: upper and lower. San Antonio's hard water buries the lower element in mineral scale and burns it out early. We test both elements and replace whichever has failed, or both if needed.

Faulty Upper or Lower Thermostat

Electric heaters have two thermostats, one for each element. A failed thermostat causes the corresponding element to stop working. Depending on which one fails, you'll get no hot water or very limited hot water.

Tripped High-Limit Reset

The high-limit safety switch cuts power to the elements if the water gets too hot. If it has tripped, there is usually an underlying cause, such as a stuck thermostat, that also needs to be addressed. Simply resetting the button without finding the root cause means it will trip again.

Wiring & Connection Issues

Loose or corroded wiring connections at the element or thermostat can cause intermittent heating or complete failure. We inspect all electrical connections as part of our electric water heater diagnostic.

Breaker Problems

A tripped breaker is the first thing to check. But if the breaker keeps tripping, there is an electrical fault somewhere in the heater circuit. We diagnose the root cause so it doesn't keep happening.

Scale-Induced Element Failure

In San Antonio, scale buildup around heating elements is the number one cause of premature electric water heater failure. We remove failed elements, clean mounting threads, and install new elements rated for high-mineral environments.

Choosing the Right Unit

Tank vs. Tankless Water Heaters in San Antonio

Both types are common across San Antonio. They fail in different ways, cost different amounts to maintain, and suit different households. Here's what you need to know.

Traditional Tank Heater

  • Lower upfront cost to purchase and install
  • Simpler mechanics, easier to repair
  • Works well for households with steady demand
  • Sediment buildup is fastest in SA hard water
  • Standby heat loss costs money 24/7
  • Typical lifespan: 8-12 years (less without maintenance)
  • Hot water is limited to tank capacity

In San Antonio, an unmaintained tank unit can fail in as little as 6 years. With professional flushes and anode rod checks, many last 12 or more.

Tankless (On-Demand) Heater

  • Endless hot water, no running out mid-shower
  • No standby heat loss, lower energy bills
  • Longer lifespan: 15-20 years with maintenance
  • Compact, saves space in utility closets
  • Higher upfront purchase and install cost
  • SA hard water requires annual descaling to protect heat exchanger
  • More complex to diagnose and repair

San Antonio's warm groundwater temperature actually makes tankless units more efficient here. Water entering the unit is warmer than in colder states, so the unit works less to reach target temperature.

Not sure which type is right for you? When we visit for a repair or replacement, we give you our honest read based on your household size, hot water demand, and budget. No pressure, no upsell.

Making the Right Call

Should You Repair or Replace Your Water Heater?

This is the most common question we get. Here is a simple, honest framework to help you decide.

Lean Toward Repair If...

  • Your unit is under 8 years old
  • The problem is a single replaceable part (thermocouple, element, thermostat, anode rod, T&P valve)
  • The repair cost is less than half the cost of a new unit
  • The tank itself is not leaking from the body
  • This is the first repair the unit has needed

Lean Toward Replacement If...

  • Your unit is over 10 to 12 years old (less in SA without maintenance)
  • The tank is leaking from the tank body (this is never repairable)
  • You've had multiple repairs in the past 12 to 24 months
  • Your energy bills keep rising despite repairs
  • Repair cost is more than half the cost of a new unit
  • Rusty or discolored hot water (internal corrosion)

One situation where replacement is not optional: A leak from the tank body itself cannot be patched or repaired. The interior has corroded through. Attempting a repair is a waste of money and a safety risk. If your tank is leaking from the body, we will tell you plainly: it's time for a new one.

A San Antonio note on age: The national average lifespan is 8 to 12 years, but in San Antonio, hard water shortens that without maintenance. An unmaintained 8-year-old unit here may already be near end of life. A well-maintained 12-year-old unit may still have good years left. Maintenance history matters just as much as age.

Act Fast

Your Water Heater Is Leaking Right Now. Do This.

Every minute a tank leaks, water damage gets worse. Do these four things while you wait for us to arrive.

  1. 1

    Turn Off the Power or Gas

    Electric unit: Go to your breaker panel and switch off the breaker labeled "water heater."
    Gas unit: Turn the gas control valve on the unit to "Pilot" or "Off."

  2. 2

    Shut Off the Cold Water Supply

    There is a cold water shutoff valve on the pipe going into the top of your water heater. Turn it clockwise until it stops. This cuts off the water supply so the leak stops getting worse.

  3. 3

    Note Where the Leak Is Coming From

    Is it from a pipe connection at the top? A valve on the side? The bottom of the tank? The drain valve? Knowing this helps us arrive prepared with the right parts. Tell us when you call.

  4. 4

    Call Us Right Now

    Do not put a bucket under it and "wait and see." A leaking tank can fail completely and flood your utility closet, garage, or attic. Call immediately.
